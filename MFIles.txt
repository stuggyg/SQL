Here are ten questions pitched for a vendor conversation or internal M-Files review — technical enough to expose real capability, but worded the way a business stakeholder would ask them.

Workflows

How does state-based workflow handle automatic transitions — can we trigger a state change from a metadata condition (e.g. a review date passing) rather than relying on a person to click, and what happens if the condition is met while the object is checked out?
When a workflow drives our retention lifecycle — opening, closing, and eventually destroying a records aggregation — can the workflow itself enforce that a closed aggregation can't be reopened or edited, or is that governed separately by permissions?

PII

How does M-Files identify and classify documents containing PII — is it native metadata-driven classification, the Discovery/intelligence add-on, or does it need a third-party classifier, and how does that work with our 26.2 build?
Once a document is flagged as containing PII, what can the platform do automatically — restrict permissions, apply retention, redact, or route to a workflow — versus what needs a manual step?

Duplicates

How does M-Files prevent duplicates at the point of capture — does it detect a duplicate on content (hashing the file) or only on matching metadata, and can it block the save or just warn the user?
For duplicates already sitting in the vault, what's the supported way to find and merge or remove them without breaking existing references, version history, or workflow state?

Exports

When we export documents out of M-Files, what comes with them — do we get the metadata and audit trail alongside the file, and in what format (native, PDF, structured XML/CSV for the metadata)?
Is there a supported bulk or scheduled export for records that have reached end-of-retention, and does that export preserve enough evidence to prove chain of custody for a disposition?

Audits

What does the audit trail actually capture out of the box — reads/views as well as edits, permission changes, workflow transitions, and deletions — and how far back is it retained by default?
Can we report on and export the audit history for a specific document or aggregation for a compliance or legal request, and can that be locked down so the audit log itself can't be altered or purged by an administrator?



Here are three, same technical-but-approachable framing:

What are the recommended CPU, RAM, and disk specs for our expected size — and how do those scale? If we're at, say, 500 users and a few hundred GB of documents today but expect to double, at what point do we outgrow a single server and need to split the M-Files Server, the SQL Server database, and full-text search onto separate machines?
How should the underlying storage be configured for performance? Specifically, what IOPS and disk latency does M-Files expect for the SQL database, the file vault data, and the search index — and are there VM-level settings (thin vs. thick provisioning, memory ballooning, CPU over-commit on the host) that you've seen quietly kill performance in virtualised deployments?
How do we validate that performance is actually healthy, and what does a slow day look like? What are the key metrics we should be monitoring — check-in/check-out times, search response, workflow processing lag — and does M-Files provide tooling to baseline and alert on these, or do we need to build that monitoring ourselves?
